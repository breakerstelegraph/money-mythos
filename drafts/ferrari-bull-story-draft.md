### [Vittoria, Sicily — historical accuracy / research notes]

By Italian standards this is a real city. Eighth-largest in Sicily today. In the 1950s it is already the produce engine of the southeast: wine, olive oil, and especially primaticci — early vegetables (tomato, pepper, zucchini, eggplant) that leave the plain before anyone else's.

Accurate character of the place: a working market city with a rural hinterland. Farmers in the contrade. Grocers, carrettieri, and casari in town. The sea 15–20 minutes south at Scoglitti. Baroque hill cities (Ragusa, Modica) 25–30 km inland and up.

Write the cart-as-chariot scene at 11–14 (1952–55). Carretto still everyday, not nostalgic. Ape exists on the island but has not replaced the painted horse yet. That's the honest decade split you were asking for.

Don't over-folklore the cart into a parade float. In 1953 it is sweaty, useful, and also a masterpiece. That's the whole point — same as Ferrari as work truck.
